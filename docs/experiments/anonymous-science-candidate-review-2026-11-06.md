# Anonymous science candidate review (Opus5.5, 2026-11-06)

The requested model was verified as claude-opus-5-5. This is a review of fixed candidate construction methods, with no benchmark questions, answers, measurements, identities or private coordinates sent. Tools were disabled and hidden reasoning was not persisted. It is not a measured ranking of the candidate checkpoints.

## A

A native scalar/vector edit (norm gains, biases, scales) targeted at the numerical folds, with contract held to a no-harm floor. Five concerns. (1) The radius of 0.025*abs(W) covers only a few BF16 ulps per coordinate, since BF16 relative spacing is roughly 0.4-0.8%. The realized edit is therefore a lattice point, not the continuous minimizer. The retained unmet linear targets and realized norm decide whether the +0.05 nats was even reachable; if shortfall is large, A is close to a quantized random nudge in the target direction. (2) Norm and scale coordinates act multiplicatively on whole channels or tokens. A 'small' change can shift logit temperature or calibration broadly, so broad code, tools, instruction and reasoning calibration may move more than the edit size suggests. (3) If both numerical folds were used in fitting, there is no within-calibration holdout. Cross-fold agreement only shows the edit fits both folds, not that it transfers. (4) A contract floor of >= -0.01 nats on a mean log-prob margin can hide a few large per-item regressions. Contract correctness is often decided by single tokens. (5) The objective is a teacher-forced correct-minus-foil margin on the public answer only. Gains may come from lowering foil probability rather than raising correct-answer probability. They may also not survive generation, where the edit also perturbs the thinking trace.

**Cheapest falsification:** In the single-load gate, report each numerical fold separately with per-item paired deltas against the original. Do the same for a per-item distribution of contract deltas, not just the mean. Reject A if either numerical fold's realized margin gain falls within the original-vs-restored-original noise band. Also reject if any contract item drops sharply, or if the gain is mostly foil suppression with flat or falling correct-answer log prob. A follow-up kill test: greedy-generate the numerical calibration tasks and sandbox-execute them; no pass-rate movement means no coding claim.

## B

The mirror of A: contract is targeted and numerical is held at a floor. The same quantization-lattice and multiplicative-scope concerns apply. Contract and state correctness failures in generation are often structural: a missing invariant check, wrong mutation order, or unhandled edge state. Mean answer log-prob margins capture these poorly, because the decisive tokens are a small fraction of the scored answer. The 'mean' part of the objective dilutes them. B also has the highest risk of looking good on the metric while actually learning surface features of the foils, such as their length or style. Because B is a component of G, B's isolated reading is needed to interpret G at all.

**Cheapest falsification:** Same paired per-fold gate, plus one diagnostic: compute the margin change restricted to tokens that differ between correct and foil versus tokens they share. If the gain sits mainly on shared or style tokens, or on the foil side only, treat B as not addressing contract logic. Then run sandbox execution on the contract calibration tasks with generated (not teacher-forced) solutions; no improvement in assertion and invariant pass rate falsifies the mechanism.

## C

Balanced +0.02 on all four folds with the same native scope. This is the most conservative and least purpose-biased native recipe, and likely the smallest realized norm. The main risk is power: +0.02 nats may sit inside run-to-run and BF16 realization noise, so a null is weakly informative. Balancing four constraints in a tiny, quantized feasible set can also leave every target partly unmet, giving a diffuse edit. Check whether the retained shortfall is uniform across folds or concentrated in one purpose. If concentrated, C is effectively A or B with a smaller amplitude, not a distinct mechanism.

**Cheapest falsification:** Compare C's realized per-fold deltas with the original-vs-restored noise floor measured in the same load. If no fold clears it, C is undetectable at this scale and should not consume a benchmark slot. Also check direction cosine (or sign agreement of realized coordinate changes) with A and B. If C is approximately a convex mix of them, its information is redundant and only the stronger component needs testing.

## D

A sparse matrix-cell edit across attention and MLP projections in every layer, with numerical targets. Strengths: geometry fixed in advance, not cherry-picked, and changes large enough to survive BF16. Risks follow. (1) Up to 50% relative change at isolated cells is far outside the regime where a first-order linear target is reliable. Realized margin can differ in sign or magnitude from prediction, so realized-vs-predicted agreement must be checked per fold. (2) A single cell in a projection reads one input feature and writes one output feature. A large change on a cell touching an outlier or massive-activation channel, or an attention-sink pathway, can behave like a token-specific trigger rather than a general skill change. (3) Spreading across every decoder layer means many independent small nonlinear perturbations. Errors can compound along long generated reasoning traces even if a single teacher-forced pass looks fine. (4) One fixed random sample tests that sample, not 'matrix-cell editing'. Without a second sample seed or a matched random-sign control, a positive result cannot be attributed to the target direction rather than generic perturbation. (5) The larger budget is an observation-tool change. Any comparison with native candidates should be at matched realized-effect or matched-norm terms, not presented as like-for-like small edits.

**Cheapest falsification:** In the same load, compare D's realized per-fold margin delta with its linear prediction. Large disagreement means the local model is invalid at this radius, and the edit should be shrunk rather than benchmarked. Check broad calibration and a short greedy generation pass on executable numerical tasks for repetition, loops, format breakage or abnormal thinking length. The decisive cheap control is the same cells with a sign-randomized or target-orthogonal change of identical realized norm. If that control produces similar numerical deltas or similar broad damage, D's effect is nonspecific.

## E

The matrix scope with contract targets. All of D's sparse-cell risks apply. On top of them, contract signals are fewer and more token-localized, so a sparse large-cell solution is especially prone to fitting idiosyncrasies of the calibration foils. E is not a component of G, so the joint candidate does not directly test it. E's specificity rests entirely on its own controls and cross-fold behavior.

**Cheapest falsification:** Paired per-fold contract deltas plus the token-localization check described for B. Run the matched random-sign control on the same cells. Then do generated-solution sandbox execution on the contract calibration fold not dominating the fit. Kill E if the less-fit fold or generation does not move, or if broad instruction-following or tool-use calibration degrades beyond the owner's tolerance.

## F

The matrix scope with balanced +0.02 targets. Because the per-cell bound is large but targets are small, F may realize a modest change, the most defensible matrix candidate on side-effect grounds. However, the same sampled cells must satisfy four constraints. Any fold where the shortfall is large signals the sampled geometry cannot express that purpose, which is itself useful negative information about the sample. As with C, a small target risks a null that is inside noise.

**Cheapest falsification:** Check per-fold realized deltas against the measured noise floor and against the retained shortfall. Compare broad-calibration movement against D and E. If F shows comparable broad damage to D or E with smaller target gains, the cells, not the targets, drive side effects, which implicates the sampled geometry. Matched random-sign control on the same cells, as for D.

## G

A joint realization of D and B, built correctly from the original base with no additivity assumption. Disjoint parameter tensors do not imply independent effects. Both edits act on the same residual stream, and native norm or scale changes rescale the inputs that D's matrix cells read. Their interaction could be multiplicative. G mixes two scopes and two purposes, so a positive G result cannot be attributed. A negative G result is ambiguous unless D and B are each read in the same load. G is the most expensive candidate to explain and should be shortlisted only if it beats the better component on the owner's total objective without extra broad loss.

**Cheapest falsification:** Using the same load, compute the interaction term: G minus D minus B plus original, per fold and on broad calibration. If G does not exceed max(D, B) on the combined numerical-plus-contract criterion, drop it. If the interaction term is large and negative on broad calibration, the components conflict. If it is near zero, G adds no mechanism beyond its parts and should be deferred.

## H

H is a fixed native edit from a broader implementation/algorithm calibration, kept as a control with no assumed transfer. It is valuable as an anchor: it tests whether the gate can detect a known edit, and whether purpose-specific targeting beats a general coding edit. Risks: H was selected on a different calibration, so any overlap between that calibration and the broad code calibration used in this gate turns H's broad-code numbers into known-reference scores, not independent transfer. Report that distinction explicitly. H also comes with its own selection history (winner's curse), so its prior positive readings will tend to shrink.

**Cheapest falsification:** Read H in the same load against the original on numerical and contract folds, which it was not fit to. No movement, or movement within the noise floor, confirms 'no positive transfer' and keeps H purely as a reference. Audit H's original calibration items and families against the gate's broad calibration and the reserved evaluation families; any overlap demotes the affected readings to known-reference status.

## Suggested sequence

- Integrity pre-check, before any scoring: verify each of the eight BF16 sets loads exactly as specified (tensor hashes, changed-coordinate counts, realized norms, max relative change, retained unmet targets). Verify the restored original is bit-identical to the original. Run the original at both ends of the load to measure the drift and nondeterminism noise floor for every metric.
- Single-load teacher-forced gate: original, then A through H in any fixed order, then restored original. Score per fold and per item on the numerical and contract calibration, plus broad code, tools, instruction and reasoning calibration. Report each fold separately, with correct-side and foil-side log prob separately as well as the margin. Report realized-versus-linear-predicted deltas and any non-finite values. Use paired comparisons and correct for choosing among eight candidates.
- Interpretation rules fixed before reading results: a candidate counts as passing only if every targeted fold clears the noise floor in the right direction, no-harm floors hold per item as well as on the mean, and broad losses stay within the owner's stated tolerance. G must beat max(D, B); C and F must not be redundant with their purpose-targeted siblings; H is read only as a control.
- Cheap specificity controls for any matrix candidate that passes: the same sampled cells with sign-randomized or target-orthogonal changes of equal realized norm. If budget allows, one replicate with a fresh fixed cell sample. Without these, a matrix result cannot be attributed to the target direction.
- Cheap generation gate before expensive scoring: greedy or fixed-seed generation on a small executable calibration subset, run in the sandbox, for the passing candidates and the original. Record pass rate, compile and runtime errors, assertion failures on contract tasks, termination and format rates, and thinking-length distribution. Drop any candidate whose teacher-forced gain does not appear in executed solutions, or that degrades termination or format.
- First expensive benchmark: at most two distinct mechanisms. Take the best passing native-scope candidate (default prior C for minimal change and balanced purpose, replaced by A or B if only one purpose clears noise) and the best passing matrix-scope candidate (default prior D or F, only if its matched-random control is clearly weaker). Include G only if it has a clear non-additive gain. Keep H as a reference reading, not a shortlist slot, unless it is the strongest on the generation gate. Run public code generation, sandbox execution and official scoring on reserved families only, with the original measured under identical decoding settings.
- Follow-up instead of more scalar amplitude grids: an exact full low-rank-donor directional derivative is the better next tool. It is better if the donor's provenance is clean, the derivative is computed exactly (not finite-differenced through BF16), and it is evaluated per fold for sign agreement. A grid along a fixed direction only probes one dimension and mostly re-measures curvature and quantization. An exact derivative screens a structured dense direction cheaply and avoids sparse large-cell pathologies by spreading change across many weights. It must still be followed by realization at two or three amplitudes, using residual correction so small per-element changes are not rounded away, then the same teacher-forced and generation gates, because a derivative is local and teacher-forced and is not a performance claim. Record donor training-data provenance to rule out overlap with the reserved evaluation families.

## Limits

- No benchmark measurements, model identity or calibration results are supplied, so this review covers methodology only and does not predict any score or ranking.
- Teacher-forced correct-minus-foil margins on public answers do not score hidden reasoning. They can rise through foil suppression, style or length cues, or shared-token effects, and they do not capture exposure effects over long generated thinking traces. Only sandbox-executed generations support coding-improvement claims.
- Linear targets and their shortfall are local first-order quantities. At 50% relative change on sparse matrix cells, and at the few-ulp lattice of native coordinates, realized effects can differ in magnitude or sign from predictions.
- With two folds per purpose, both likely used in fitting, calibration agreement measures fit consistency, not transfer. Family-separated evaluation is the only transfer evidence, and it weakens with each selection decision made after seeing calibration results.
- Choosing among eight candidates on small calibration sets produces winner's-curse inflation; the shortlisted candidates' gate readings should be expected to shrink on fresh data.
- A single fixed matrix cell sample tests that sample only. Conclusions about matrix-cell editing as a mechanism need matched random controls or a second sample.
- The matrix budget is an observation-tool change. Comparisons between native and matrix scopes should be framed at matched realized norm or effect, and the larger budget does not alter official grading.
- Readings on calibration that overlaps H's original calibration, or any donor's training data, are known-reference scores, not independent transfer.
- Moderate unrelated losses should be reported per capability with paired item-level detail, so that acceptance under the owner's total coding objective is transparent rather than averaged away.

## Further confirmation suggestions

- The family-separated evaluation tasks, untouched by any fitting, selection, threshold-setting or debugging decision until the shortlist is frozen.
- A fresh executable calibration fold per purpose (numerical and contract), held back from the eight-candidate gate. Used once to re-validate the shortlisted candidates before the expensive benchmark.
- A fresh fixed matrix cell sample, under a different predeclared seed, to replicate any passing matrix candidate.
- Matched random-sign or target-orthogonal control edits for each passing scope, at equal realized norm, generated without looking at calibration outcomes.
- Separate fresh folds reserved for the low-rank-donor directional-derivative follow-up, so its screening does not reuse data already spent on selecting among A through H.
- A small broad-capability holdout (code, tools, instruction-following, reasoning) not used in the gate, to confirm that the accepted side-effect levels hold on unseen items.

## Owner decisions

Use finite, BF16-realized effects and generated-code execution before claiming improvement. A native and a matrix candidate provide distinct first WSP tests; a joint set must be compared with its components. Large relative sparse-cell changes require scrutiny for nonlinear and broad-capability damage. The existing known reference and prospective confirmation sets remain labeled by their actual history; this review does not make them unseen. Moderate unrelated losses remain permitted by the user and are reported per capability. No new blanket zero-regression condition is imposed. Exact low-rank donor directional derivatives are a potential follow-up, with unresolved donor provenance and training overlap disclosed.
